# Code of conduct

Threads of History is a map of how historical events connect, built in public. Its discussions are about evidence. These rules apply in GitHub Discussions, issues, pull requests and anywhere else the project meets.

## The rules

1. **Bring a source.** A claim about what happened, or about what caused what, needs a source that states it: a document, a scholarly work, a court record, a reputable news report. "Everyone knows" and "it's obvious" are not sources. Quote the sentence that supports you and link to it.
2. **Discuss the evidence, not each other.** Disagree with a claim by showing a better source or a flaw in the reading. No insults, no guessing at people's motives, no piling on.
3. **Contested means contested.** Where serious people disagree, the project records each side and who holds it; it does not pick a winner. Argue for adding a well-sourced position, not for silencing the other one.
4. **History, not today's politics for its own sake.** Present-day events belong here when they connect to the map through sources. Campaigning, party talking points and culture-war fights do not.
5. **Care with real people.** Name people only with the role a source gives them: convicted, charged, accused (by whom), investigated, cleared. Being photographed with someone, or appearing in a log, is not an accusation. No posting private information about anyone.
6. **No hate, harassment or threats**, including against groups, and including denial or celebration of atrocities. Discussing difficult history is welcome; using it to attack people is not.
7. **Good faith.** Don't post knowingly false material, sockpuppet, or brigade.

## Moderation

Moderators are people, not automated systems. They may edit titles, move or lock discussions, hide comments that break these rules, and, for repeated or serious breaches, block an account. Contested topics (such as Israel–Palestine, immigration, or political scandals) may be put in slow mode or limited to sourced comments.

To report a problem, use GitHub's "Report content" on the comment, or open a discussion in **Ideas** titled "Moderation:" if it is about how the rules work. Reports about a specific person's conduct can be sent to the maintainers privately through GitHub's report feature.
